# Estimator baselines: Ridge vs split conformal vs gradient boosting

Generated by `backend/scripts/baseline_comparison.py` on 2026-10-24.
Deterministic; the dataset is `data/synthetic_history.csv` (sha1 `b1df6f1b…`).

**Question.** Is the deployed Ridge + conformal-style interval the right estimator
for this problem, or would a true split-conformal interval, a gradient-boosted
model, or quantile regression do better on the same 218 walk-forward steps?

## Method

- Same leakage-free protocol as the replay: for `i = 10..n-1`, train on
  `rows[:i]` (recorded features), predict row `i`.
- `ridge_style`: the deployed Ridge (α=0.3, log target) + out-of-fold residual
  quantiles + finite-sample correction + 1.10 tail widening.
- `ridge_split`: Ridge fit on the first 80% of the training window and
  calibrated on the most recent 20% (true split conformal; no tail widening).
- `gbm_split`: `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` (200 iterations, depth 3) with
  the same split-conformal calibration.
- `gbm_quant`: gradient-boosted quantile regression at α = 0.1/0.5/0.9
  (200 estimators, depth 2), no conformal step.
- `median`: constant median of the fit window with empirical 10/90 quantiles.

Metrics: MAE of P50 (days), empirical P10–P90 coverage, relative width
`(P90-P10)/P50`.

## Results (218 steps)

| Method | MAE (days) | Coverage | Rel. width |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `ridge_style` (deployed) | **17.36** | **86.7%** | **0.83** |
| `ridge_split` (true split conformal) | 17.25 | 75.7% | 1.04 |
| `gbm_split` | 25.23 | 72.9% | 2.05 |
| `gbm_quant` | 25.56 | 66.5% | 0.98 |
| `median` | 32.76 | 77.1% | 1.77 |

## Reading

1. **Ridge is the best estimator on this sample.** The gradient-boosted models
   are 45–47% worse in MAE; at n≈200 with 15 binary features, extra flexibility
   buys variance, not signal.
2. **The deployed interval is the best calibrated option.** It has the highest
   coverage (86.7%) and the narrowest relative width (0.83). True split
   conformal is slightly better on MAE (17.25) but wider (1.04) and under-covers
   on the chronological split (75.7%, consistent with a small exchangeability
   violation from the productivity drift).
3. **The median baseline is not competitive on MAE** (32.76), but its empirical
   interval still covers 77.1%: a reminder that interval coverage alone is not
   evidence of a useful point estimate.

## Caveats

- Synthetic data-generating process; recorded features (the oracle-feature
  setting), not extracted features.
- The 80/20 fit/calibration split is chronological; conformal exchangeability is
  approximate under drift, which is part of what the split-conformal row shows.
- Single deterministic seed; the demo is small (218 steps), so differences of a
  few tenths of a day are within noise.
- `gbm_quant` can produce crossing quantiles; the reported interval is the raw
  q10/q90 (no rearrangement).

## Additional checks (2026-10-24, after an external review)

**De-biased team baseline.** The synthetic team estimate is generated with a
0.75 optimism factor and weakened integration/migration coefficients, so its
raw MAE (32.93) is not a fair human baseline. Multiplying each team estimate by
the walk-forward median actual/team ratio yields a **de-biased team MAE of
18.26**; the estimator's 17.36 is about 5% better, not 47%.

**JOSSE project-level split.** The within-project split in the paper's main
text lets the model see each test project in training. With 80% of the 371
project prefixes as training projects and 20% held out, text+metadata reaches
**MAE 2.76 h vs 2.95 h for the global median (+6.4%)**, MedAE 0.55 vs 0.83,
coverage 71.2%; experts win on the 945 test rows where both exist (6.07 vs
7.45 h). The within-project split gives the more optimistic 13.1% (3.32 vs
3.82 h).
