# Unknown-feature handling: Monte-Carlo ablation

Generated by `backend/scripts/mc_ablation.py` on 2026-10-23 15:27 UTC.
Deterministic (fixed seeds); the dataset is `data/synthetic_history.csv` (sha1 `b1df6f1b6712...`). Re-running reproduces these numbers.

> **Scope note (2026-10-24).** This ablation covers **uniform/random masking**,
> the MCAR regime. Under that mechanism the marginal prevalence is the right
> prior and Monte Carlo restores coverage. The **deployment** missingness is
> different: an extraction `null` means "not mentioned", and only 4.1% of null
> cells are positive (marginal 25.2%). On that path the global-prevalence
> sampler is harmful and a conditional prior is required; see
> `docs/ENDTOEND_REPLAY.md` (zero-fill 21.34 / global 49.64 / conditional 20.48
> MAE). Both results are valid for their own mechanism; do not apply one to the
> other.

**Question.** When the extraction leaves a feature unresolved
(`null`), how should the estimator handle it? The product samples
unknown features with 2,000 independent Bernoulli draws from
training-set prevalence plus an out-of-fold residual bootstrap (D11).
This ablation compares that choice against zero-fill, a local
prevalence variant, k-NN donor sampling, and the oracle.

## Method

- Walk-forward replay: for `i = 10..n-1`, train on `rows[:i]` only (no leakage), predict row `i`.
- Unknowns are simulated by masking each feature with probability `q` (at least one hidden); 3 mask seeds per step; 218 steps per q.
- Metrics: empirical coverage of the P10–P90 interval, relative width `(P90-P10)/P50`, and P50 MAE.
- `k = 15` nearest historical rows (Hamming distance on known features).
- `2000` Monte-Carlo draws per arm and evaluation.

## Results

### Unknown rate `q = 20%`

| arm | coverage | rel. width | MAE (P50) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `zero` | 61.2% | 0.83 | 26.18 |
| `indep` | 85.6% | 1.15 | 22.14 |
| `local` | 84.1% | 1.13 | 22.40 |
| `knn` | 83.6% | 1.12 | 22.34 |
| `oracle` | 86.7% | 0.83 | 17.36 |

### Unknown rate `q = 40%`

| arm | coverage | rel. width | MAE (P50) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `zero` | 39.3% | 0.83 | 34.46 |
| `indep` | 85.2% | 1.41 | 25.38 |

| `knn` | 83.9% | 1.35 | 25.50 |
| `oracle` | 86.7% | 0.83 | 17.36 |

## Reading

- **Zero-fill collapses coverage.** Treating an unresolved feature as absent produces 61.2% (q=20%) and 39.3% (q=40%) coverage: the interval is too narrow and the miss is systematic.
- **Independent Monte Carlo is the best practical arm** (85.6% / 85.2% coverage, lowest P50 MAE), close to the oracle (86.7%) while widening the interval honestly as the unknown rate grows (rel. width 1.15 → 1.41).
- **Correlation-aware variants did not help.** Local prevalence and k-NN donor sampling are slightly worse on coverage and MAE; on this data-generating process the independent draws plus the residual bootstrap are sufficient, and the extra variance from neighbour sampling is not repaid.
- **Coverage is stable when unknowns double** (20% → 40%), which is the behaviour the product claims: missing information widens the range instead of silently narrowing it.

## Caveats

- The evaluation uses the synthetic DGP and *random* masks; real extraction errors may be correlated with the text rather than uniform.
- `k = 15` is a fixed choice; tuning it is unlikely to close the small gap, but was not exhaustively searched.
- The oracle is an upper bound, not a deployable method (it uses the hidden truth).

## Paper-ready method paragraph (EN)

> Unresolved features are not imputed as absent. For each estimate, the model draws 2,000 completed binary vectors by sampling every unknown feature from its training-set prevalence, adds an out-of-fold conformal residual to each draw, and reports the empirical P10/P50/P90 of the resulting distribution. On the walk-forward replay this restores 85.6% / 85.2% interval coverage at 20% / 40% unknown rates, versus 61.2% / 39.3% when unknowns are zero-filled, and comes within 1.1 / 1.5 points of the oracle (86.7%). Correlation-aware variants (local prevalence and k-NN donor sampling) did not improve coverage or error on this DGP, so the simpler independent sampler is kept and its independence assumption is reported as a limitation.
